## P-20 — `setSyncData` had no caller

`midi.ts:336` defined it; nothing in `src/`, `e2e/` or `scripts/` called it, and
`git log -S` finds no caller at any point in this repository's history. So the
nearest-beat fold at `midi.ts:654-662` was unreachable production code.

Removed: `setSyncData`, the `_sharedBuffer` and `_correlator` fields, the
`HitEvent.deltaMs` field that only this path ever wrote, and the imports left
dangling.

**The half that mattered is `DrillSession`.** It seeded its delta from
`hit.deltaMs` and recomputed only `if (correlator)`. Since `deltaMs` was
permanently `0`, and `0` categorises as a perfect GREEN, a missing correlator
would have lit every hit green while the app measured nothing — register P-10
exactly, one null away. It now suppresses feedback and logs why, because showing
a drummer nothing is honest and showing them a perfect hit they did not play is
not.

**Deliberately not done: `nearestBeatDeltaMs` is kept.** The register entry said
"delete the dead path or wire it", and I deleted the path but not this. It has
ten unit tests and an `audioworklet.spec.ts` test that reads the live SAB the
worklet publishes, so it is not the untested orphan the entry implies. And
folding to the nearest *beat* is not what grading wants: `DrillSession` measures
against the drill's own note targets, which a beat fold cannot do for offbeat
notes. Deleting eleven passing tests for tidiness, days before a hardware
session, is the wrong trade. Documented in place at the function.

## P-21 — `dynamicContrastDb` was live dead data

A **required** field on `PassCriteria`, declared by all ten drills with
meaningful-looking values — `dynamics-gate.ts` asked for 15 dB — and read by
nothing in `src/workers/` or `src/session/`. A reader of the content files would
reasonably conclude drill 3 enforced 15 dB of contrast.

Removed from the type and all 24 declarations. The reasoning for why it cannot
come back is kept rather than deleted with it, in `types.ts` and
`dynamicsCalibration.ts`: decibels describe the sound the module makes, the app
receives a 7-bit velocity through a curve it cannot see, and measuring dB needs a
microphone. T-046 already carries the intent in the unit actually available.

## P-22 — the capability check ignored the hi-hat pedal

`checkHardwareCapability` warned about `snare-rim` and nothing else. All nine
`hihat-chick` notes are in the Hi-Hat Independence bootcamp, and a chick normally
arrives as a **controller**, not a note, so `hasSeenNote` could not see it. The
pedal's *value* cannot stand in either: a pedal resting fully open reads 0, which
is indistinguishable from no pedal at all.

The consequence was specific and expensive. A drummer whose pedal sends a
controller we have not identified — which C-55 exists because modules do — plays
`hh-indep-1` and is told **"You missed 9 of 16 notes."** They then debug their
playing, or their pedal, for something the app knew before they pressed Start.

`midiEngine` now records whether this kit's pedal controller has spoken, and the
warning that already existed fires for the pedal. Modules that send a physical
note 44 still count, so controller silence alone is not a false alarm.

## P-23 — hi-hat calibration was unreachable after first run

`isCalibrationOpen` was set from exactly two places: first launch with no stored
calibration, and the end of placement. Settings offered "Map My Kit" and
"Calibrate Dynamics" but nothing for the hi-hat. A drummer who cancelled it, or
whose pedal was identified wrongly, had no way back short of clearing site data —
and pedal *detection* rides on that screen, so this is where a kit whose pedal is
not on the conventional controller gets identified at all.

Added beside the other two calibrations, where it belongs.

## P-26 — the app was constrained to 1126px

Found by T-056 on its first run; the measurements, reasoning and mutation check
are in [T-056.md](T-056.md). In short: the Preact starter's `#app` rule centred
the app in a 1126px column while `ThroneView` is `100vw`, so the QuickMenu was
carried entirely off-screen at 1920 and 3440.

## D5 — a browser without Web MIDI now says so

The app reached the kit step, listened to a MIDI stack that could never deliver
anything, and after six seconds offered *"No kit connected — continue anyway"* —
sending a Safari user to check a cable for a limitation of their browser. Web
MIDI does not exist in WebKit, so this is every browser on iPhone and iPad too.

It now names the real problem and what does work, and offers the way forward
immediately rather than after a six-second wait for a kit that could never
answer.

### Notes for review

**My own test was wrong first, and it taught me something worth recording.** The
D5 test began with `delete navigator.requestMIDIAccess`, which is a no-op —
`requestMIDIAccess` lives on `Navigator.prototype`, so the instance has no own
property to delete. The real API stayed in place and the app failed on
*permission* instead, with `NotAllowedError`. Deleting from the prototype is the
honest removal.

That surfaced **a third state neither D5 nor this change handles: Web MIDI
present but permission denied.** The app says the same thing it says for no kit.
The remedy is completely different — grant the permission — so the message is
wrong in a way that will cost someone time. Not fixed here because it needs the
init failure plumbed from `app.tsx` into `EngineWarmup`, which is a wider change
than this batch. **Worth filing.**

**P-24 and P-25 found while reading, not fixed.** `DrillRunner.ts:171` records a
deliberate tab-hide as `error: 'audio-stall'`, which is the one signal being used
to track P-1, so switching windows mid-drill adds a false data point to the
investigation. And the result stats line is 18px at `#666` on near-black, which is
the least legible thing on a screen read from 1.5–2.5m. Both are judgement calls
that touch things other people are relying on — the audit guards on that error
value — so they are reported rather than changed.

**`checkHardwareCapability` still only *warns*.** A drill whose pedal has never
been seen is playable, because the drummer may simply not have touched it yet.
That is the right default, but it means the warning is the only protection and it
can be walked past. If the hardware session shows people walking past it, the
answer is probably to require one pedal movement before Start on drills that need
it — not to make the warning louder.
