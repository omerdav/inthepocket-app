### Why this worked where T-036 did not

Both attempts had the same idea — freeze the clock so the render state is
deterministic. The difference is **where the clock is installed**.

T-036:
```ts
await page.clock.install({ time: 0 });
await page.goto('/?dev=1');
await dismissFirstRun(page);
```

T-041:
```ts
await page.goto('/?dev=1');
await dismissFirstRun(page);
await page.clock.install({ time: 1000 });
```

T-036 froze time **before navigation**, so the entire page load ran under a
controlled clock — fonts, layout and first paint all settling against a clock
that was not advancing normally. That is why removing its `maxDiffPixelRatio`
left it failing 3 runs in 8: the tolerance was hiding a race the freeze had
moved rather than removed, and the moved race was in page setup.

Installing the clock after the page has settled freezes only the thing that
needs freezing — the glow's decay between the hit and the screenshot.

### The evidence that pointed here

Three failures at exactly **497** pixels, then — after T-036's freeze — three
more at exactly **481**. A stable pixel count across failures is not
anti-aliasing noise; it is two discrete render states. The count *changing*
when the clock moved proved the freeze was affecting what was drawn without
resolving which state you got. That is what identified page setup rather than
glow decay as the remaining variable.
